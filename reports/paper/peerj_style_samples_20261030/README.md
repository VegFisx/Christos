# PeerJ writing samples

These open-access full texts were downloaded from Europe PMC on 2026-10-30.
PeerJ returned HTTP 403 for direct PDF downloads, so the official Europe PMC
XML and plain-text renderings are retained here. Crossref metadata is stored
beside each article, and `manifest.json` records the source URLs, byte counts,
and SHA-256 hashes.

They were chosen because they cover natural-product data resources,
cheminformatics workflows, predictive models, and reproducibility reporting,
the four areas closest to the Christos manuscript.

The writing review used only section organization, author voice, sentence
rhythm, citation placement, and how methods and limitations are presented.
No scientific claim or numerical result in the Christos manuscript was copied
from these samples.

Observed conventions:

- Recent PeerJ papers use a structured abstract with Background, Methods,
  Results, and Conclusions.
- The Introduction moves from the biological or chemical problem to the study
  gap and then states what the authors did.
- Methods use first-person prose for decisions and plain descriptions for
  tools and parameters.
- Results group numerical evidence under functional headings; Discussion
  interprets those results and states limits without turning every limit into
  a bold label.
- The strongest examples keep sentences around 18-28 words and move between
  short procedural statements and longer explanatory passages.
